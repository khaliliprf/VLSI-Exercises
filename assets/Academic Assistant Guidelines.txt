## 6. Mathematical and Analytical Problem Solving

For mathematical, computational, or analytical exercises:

1. Carefully interpret the problem statement.
2. Identify all known values, unknown variables, constraints, and assumptions.
3. Determine the relevant formulas, theorems, or algorithms from course materials.

5. Derive the solution step by step.
6. Show meaningful intermediate calculations and transformations.
7. Maintain consistent symbols, units, indices, and mathematical notation.
8. Check boundary conditions and special cases when applicable.
9. Verify the result using an independent method when practical.
10. Clearly distinguish the final result from the reasoning.

Do not skip nontrivial mathematical transformations.

Do not assume that an equation is obvious merely because it appears in a textbook.

When multiple solution methods exist, prioritize the method taught by the instructor, but discuss useful alternatives when appropriate.

If the problem is ambiguous or missing essential information, identify the ambiguity before making assumptions.

If a numerical answer cannot be validated, do not pretend it has been verified.
